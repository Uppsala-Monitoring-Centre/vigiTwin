Task:
You are given two adverse event reports. Assess whether they likely describe the same individual.
Ignore whether they describe the same event, the same clinical episode, or the same time period.
Ignore all content about adverse events.

Inputs:
Report 1 and Report 2 with drugs (and dates), patient demographics (age, sex, height, weight, country), and any helpful context (e.g., report source).

Demographics for Common Drugs table summarizing how often the shared drug combination co-occurs in the database and its overall demographic profile (age distribution, gender split, countries, years).

How to reason (use two pillars):

Pillar A – Demographics
Consider age, sex, height, weight (weight may vary), and country (duplicates can cross countries via travel/multinational reporting especially for cases derived from scientific publication - so-called literature cases).

Treat major contradictions (e.g., male vs female; incompatible age like toddler vs elderly) as compelling evidence for different.

Treat close alignment (e.g., exact matches on height/weight or age) as compelling evidence of same, if drug combination is rare or treatment dates match exactly.


Pillar B – Drug-regimen similarity (including rarity/context)
Use drug names, treatment dates/timing, purpose/indication if available, and the provided “Demographics for Common Drugs” table.

Compelling for same if regimens are nearly identical and several treatment dates match (allowing for some missing values), or if the shared drug combination is rare and demographics/timing align.

Compelling for different if there are clear regimen contradictions (distinct, largely non-overlapping drug sets (each report includes several drugs not listed on the other), or incompatible treatment periods for the same drugs).

In the absence of compelling information either way, always output "unclear" (abstain). Only give "same" or "different" when you are completely sure. In case of any uncertainity be conservative and give "unclear"

Do not infer beyond available data.

Integrate both pillars when deciding: compelling evidence from either pillar may be sufficient for “different,” but both should generally support “same.”
